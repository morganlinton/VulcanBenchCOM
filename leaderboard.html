FAQ

The questions this board keeps getting.

Claude Fable 5 is shown at “low”, did you only test low?

No, sweeping every effort level is the whole idea. Fable 5 ran at low, medium, and high; low scored best (89% against 81% and 85%), so low is the row. The same holds for every model: the row shows the winner of its own ladder, and the full board lists all 42 model×effort columns, with a “best” marker on each model’s representative one.

Doesn’t more reasoning always help?

Not on this suite. Roughly half the models are flat or run backward as effort rises, Claude Opus 5 falls 87→78, Qwen3.8-Max collapses 81→55, Grok 4.6 peaks at medium. The mechanism is usually not wrong answers but time: higher effort reasons past step and wall-clock budgets, turning quick wins into unfinished runs (Reports 12, 14, and 17 trace this failure-mode shift in detail).

Why won’t you rank the models inside the error band?

With 23 tasks, one standard error is ±6 to 10 points, and twelve of the fifteen models sit within one stderr of the leader. Ordering inside that band would be reading noise as signal. What does separate models reliably is cost and speed: similar accuracy spans over 10× in price and roughly 6× in wall-clock across the best columns, and far more across the full board.

What’s the deal with the “default” markers?

Where a provider documents what an unset request gets, that column is marked on the full board, and it is never the best column. Grok 4.6 defaults to its worst measured setting (high, 74%), the Qwen 3.8 family defaults to xhigh, the bottom of both its ladders, and GLM 5.3 defaults to max, the bottom of its ladder (65% against 78% at low). Meta documents no default at all for Muse Spark 1.2, so its column carries no marker. If you integrate a model without touching reasoning_effort, this is the column you are actually running.

Why do some columns cover fewer than 23 tasks?

Partial coverage is disclosed per column: Claude Fable 5 excludes tasks its safety filters refused (19 to 21 of 23 depending on level), Claude Haiku 4.5 and Kimi K3 have provider-side gaps, and five Qwen3.8-Max xhigh runs died to API read timeouts and are excluded rather than scored zero. A partial-coverage sweep is never allowed to outrank a full one.

Method & notes

VulcanBench VulcanBench-SWE v3: 23 tasks from real merged post-cutoff PRs (Python 9, TypeScript 4, Rust 4, Go 3, JavaScript 3), graded by deterministic hidden tests in Docker with model judges disabled. Each row aggregates every fresh run of that model at that effort whose task hashes match the frozen suite; pass@1 is the mean per-task success rate and its standard error is the spread of per-task means. The cards are regenerated from the public run data with the harness’s scripts/rankings-chart pipeline; table rows are transcribed from that pipeline’s output and the numbered reports. Individual reports on the Benchmarks page carry the per-task detail, failure modes, and caveats behind each row. The whole board, all 42 columns with cost, speed, coverage, and default markers, is also published as machine-readable JSON.
